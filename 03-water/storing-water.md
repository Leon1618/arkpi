# Storing an Emergency Water Supply

Do this **now**, while tap water is safe.

## How much

- **At least 1 gallon (≈3.8 L) per person, per day, for 3 days.**
- **2 weeks' worth if you can.**
- Store more for pregnant women, people who are sick, pets, and hot climates.

| People | 3 days | 2 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 gal (≈11 L) | 14 gal (≈53 L) |
| 2 | 6 gal (≈23 L) | 28 gal (≈106 L) |
| 4 | 12 gal (≈45 L) | 56 gal (≈212 L) |

## Easiest: buy bottled water

**Unopened store-bought bottled water is the safest choice.** Check the
expiration date.

## Storing your own

**1. Pick containers**

- Best: **food-grade** water containers.
- Otherwise: a **tight-closing lid**, **unbreakable material (not glass)**, and
  a narrow opening if possible.
- **Never** use containers that held chemicals (bleach, pesticides, etc.).

**2. Clean and sanitize them**

1. Wash with soap and rinse well with water.
2. Mix **1 teaspoon of unscented household bleach in 1 quart (4 cups / ≈1 L)
   of water.** Pour it in the container.
3. Close the lid and **shake** so it touches every inside surface.
4. Wait **at least 30 seconds**, then pour it out.
5. Let the container air-dry.

**3. Fill and label**

1. Fill with clean tap water and close tightly.
2. Label it **"Drinking water"** with **today's date**.

**4. Store it**

- **Cool place: 50–70 °F (10–21 °C).**
- **Out of direct sunlight.**
- **Away from gasoline, pesticides and other chemicals.**
- **Replace it every 6 months.**

## Using stored water

- Pour it out, or use a **clean** scoop each time.
- **Don't put your hands** in the water or touch the inside of the container.

## Also keep

- A bottle of **unscented household bleach (5–9% sodium hypochlorite)** to
  disinfect water if you need to (see [making-water-safe.md](making-water-safe.md)).
