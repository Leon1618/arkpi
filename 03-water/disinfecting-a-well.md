# Disinfecting a Well After a Flood

> **Best done by a well or pump contractor.** Ask your local council or health
> department first. Until the well tests clean, **boil drinking water** (rolling
> boil, 1 minute) or use another safe source.

**Water that smells of fuel or chemicals can't be fixed with bleach.** Use bottled
water and contact the health department.

## Safety first

- **Turn off the power** to the pump before clearing debris. Have an electrician
  or pump contractor check the wiring before turning it back on.
- **Never climb into a well pit.** Dangerous gases can build up there.
- Wear **goggles or a face shield** and work where there's fresh air.
- Don't drink or bathe in the water until the job is finished.

## Steps

1. Clear debris and repair the pump if needed.
2. Pump or bail water **until it runs clear**. Let it drain outside, not into the
   house plumbing.
3. Close the valves to any water softener.
4. Mix **unscented household bleach** with **11–19 litres (3–5 gallons) of
   water** in a bucket. The amount of bleach depends on the well's depth and
   width (CDC tables; for example, a drilled well 3 m deep with a 10 cm casing
   needs about 3¼ tablespoons).
5. Pour it into the well, keeping it away from electrical connections. Circulate
   it with a hose, and rinse the inside of the casing for **5–10 minutes**.
6. Run **every indoor tap until you smell chlorine**, then turn them off and let
   the water sit for **at least 12 hours**.
7. Drain the chlorinated water **onto bare ground** (like a driveway), not into a
   creek or pond, until the chlorine smell is gone. Then run all indoor taps
   until they don't smell of chlorine.

## Test it

- Wait **7–10 days**, with all the chlorine flushed out, then have the water
  tested for **total coliforms and *E. coli***.
- Retest after **2–4 weeks** and again after **3–4 months**, then at least twice
  a year.
- Bacteria still there? Disinfect again. If it keeps failing, contact the health
  department.
