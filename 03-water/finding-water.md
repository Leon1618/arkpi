# Finding Water in an Emergency

Listen to local officials for advice about your tap water. You may need to
**shut off your home's main water valve** to keep contaminated water out of
your pipes.

## Inside your home (safe to drink)

- **Water heater tank** (the one that feeds your taps and showers). In older
  homes, **don't** use the tank that feeds radiators. That is heating system
  water, and it's not safe.
- **Melted ice cubes**, if they were made with clean water.
- **Toilet tank, not the bowl**, only if the water is clear and has no cleaning
  chemicals in it (for example, cleaners that color the water).
- **Liquid from canned fruit and vegetables.**

## Inside your home (washing only, not drinking)

- **Swimming pools and spas**: fine for washing, cleaning and personal hygiene.
  **Don't drink it.**

## Outside (must be treated before use)

All of these can carry germs from animal waste, sewage or chemicals.
**Treat them before drinking or washing**; see
[making-water-safe.md](making-water-safe.md).

- Rainwater
- Streams and rivers
- Ponds and lakes
- Natural springs
- Wells, after a flood

## Never use

> **Water contaminated with fuel or toxic chemicals cannot be made safe.**
> Don't drink it or wash with it, even after boiling or bleach.

- Toilet bowl water
- Toilet tank water with chemical cleaners in it
- Radiator or boiler water from a home heating system
