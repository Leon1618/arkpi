# Choking (Adults and Children Over 1 Year)

## Is it really choking?

Ask: **"Are you choking? Can you speak?"**

If they can **cough hard, speak or breathe**, don't interfere. Coughing is the
best way to clear it. Stay with them and be ready to act if it gets worse.

## If they can't speak, cough or breathe

Repeat **5 back blows, then 5 abdominal thrusts**, until the object comes out
or the person passes out.

**Back blows**

1. Stand behind them, supporting their chest with one arm.
2. Lean them forward until their chest is about level with the ground.
3. Give a firm blow **between the shoulder blades** with the heel of your
   other hand.

**Abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver)**

1. Stand behind them and wrap your arms around their waist.
2. Make a fist. Put the **thumb side just above the belly button**, well
   below the breastbone.
3. Grab the fist with your other hand and pull **quickly inward and upward**.

**Pregnant or very large person:** use **chest thrusts** instead. Put your
fist on the middle of the breastbone and push firmly backward.

## If they pass out

1. Shout for help, and have someone call your local emergency number.
2. Lower them carefully to a firm, flat surface.
3. **Start CPR** (see [cpr.md](cpr.md)). Chest compressions, not abdominal
   thrusts, are used for someone who is unconscious.
4. Before each set of breaths, **look in the mouth**. If you can see a
   **loose** object, take it out. Don't reach for something stuck deep in the
   throat.
5. Keep going until they breathe on their own or help arrives.

## Don't

- Don't force open the mouth of a conscious person to grab the object.
- Don't do anything while they are coughing strongly, talking or breathing well.

> Infants under 1 year need a different technique. Learn it in a first-aid
> course.
