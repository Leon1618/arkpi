# Carbon Monoxide (CO) Poisoning

Carbon monoxide has **no smell and no color**. It comes from anything that
burns fuel: generators, gas stoves and heaters, grills, car engines. The risk
is highest during power outages, when people are tempted to use these indoors.

## Signs

- **Early:** like the flu but **without a fever**: headache, tiredness,
  shortness of breath, nausea, dizziness
- **Severe:** confusion, vomiting, loss of coordination, passing out, death

## If you suspect CO, or a CO alarm goes off

1. **Get everyone outside into fresh air immediately.** Don't look for the
   source. Staying inside can be fatal.
2. **Count heads.** Make sure everyone is out.
3. **Call emergency services** (911 in the US) from outside or a neighbor's
   home.
4. **See a doctor** right away and say you suspect CO poisoning.
5. **Don't go back in** until emergency responders say it's safe.
6. Don't use the suspected appliance again until a qualified technician has
   checked it.

**Never ignore a CO alarm.** If it goes off again within 24 hours of going
back in, repeat these steps and have all fuel-burning equipment inspected.

## Prevent it

- Run generators **only outdoors**, at least **20 feet (6 m)** from windows,
  doors and attached garages.
- **Never** heat your home with a gas stove or oven.
- Put a working CO alarm (battery-powered or with battery backup) on **every
  level** of your home.
