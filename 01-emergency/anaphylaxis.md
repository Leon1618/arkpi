# Anaphylaxis (Severe Allergic Reaction)

Anaphylaxis can kill within minutes. **If in doubt, give adrenaline.** It has no
serious side effects if it turns out not to be needed.

## Signs

**Any one of these is an emergency:**

- Difficult or noisy breathing
- Swelling of the tongue
- Swelling or tightness in the throat
- Difficulty talking or a hoarse voice
- Wheeze or a cough that won't stop
- Persistent dizziness or collapse
- Young children: pale and floppy

**Milder signs that can come first:** swelling of the face, lips or eyes; hives
or welts; stomach pain or vomiting.

## What to do

1. **Give an adrenaline autoinjector** (EpiPen or similar) **straight away.**
   Follow the person's ASCIA action plan if they have one.
2. **Call 000** (112 from a mobile) and ask for an ambulance.
3. **Lay them down and keep them still.** Don't let them stand or walk, even if
   they seem better.
   - Breathing is hard or they're vomiting: **sit them up with legs out
     straight** instead.
   - Unconscious but breathing: **recovery position**.
4. **No better after 5 minutes? Give a second autoinjector** if you have one.
5. Unresponsive and not breathing normally: **start CPR** (see [cpr.md](cpr.md)).

**Asthma as well?** Give the adrenaline autoinjector **first**, then the asthma
reliever puffer.

## Don't

- Don't let them stand up or walk around.
- Don't delay the adrenaline to "wait and see".
