# CPR for Babies and Children

For adults and older children, see [cpr.md](cpr.md). **Call 000** (112 from a
mobile) first, or have someone call, and start CPR straight away.

| | Baby (under 1 year) | Child (1 year and older) |
|---|---|---|
| **Where to press** | **2 fingers** on the lower half of the breastbone, middle of the chest | **Heel of one hand** (or two) on the lower half of the breastbone |
| **How deep** | **⅓ of the chest depth** | **⅓ of the chest depth** |
| **How fast** | **100–120 per minute** | **100–120 per minute** |
| **Ratio** | **30 compressions : 2 breaths** | **30 compressions : 2 breaths** |
| **Breaths** | Your mouth over the baby's **mouth and nose** | Pinch the nose, your mouth over theirs |
| **Head** | Tilt back **very slightly** (neutral) | Tilt back and lift the chin |
| **AED (defibrillator)** | **Don't use** | Under 8: child pads if there are any, otherwise adult. Over 8: adult pads |

## Baby (under 1 year)

1. Lay the baby on their back on a firm surface.
2. **Head neutral:** tilt it back only very slightly and lift the chin. Don't
   press on the throat or neck.
3. **30 compressions:** 2 fingers on the lower half of the breastbone, press down
   one third of the chest depth, 100–120 per minute.
4. **2 breaths:** seal your mouth over the baby's **mouth and nose** and breathe
   for about **1 second**, watching for the chest to rise.
5. Repeat **30 : 2**, about 5 rounds every 2 minutes, until help arrives or the
   baby starts breathing.

## Child (1 year and older)

1. Hand on the forehead, other hand lifting the chin, to open the airway.
2. **30 compressions:** heel of one hand (two if the child is big) on the lower
   half of the breastbone, one third of the chest depth, 100–120 per minute.
   If the chest is big enough for two hands, use adult CPR.
3. **2 breaths:** pinch the nose, seal your mouth over theirs, about 1 second
   each until the chest rises.
4. Repeat **30 : 2** until help arrives or they start breathing.

**Can't give breaths?** Keep doing compressions without stopping. It may still
save a life.
