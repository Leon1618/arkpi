# Medicines in an Emergency

## Before

- Keep **medicines, prescriptions and care plans together** in a safe place you
  can grab quickly if you have to leave.
- Store them **out of reach of children**, where a broken or leaking bottle
  can't contaminate food or other medicines.
- If you rely on **refrigerated medicine** (like insulin) or powered medical
  equipment, ask your doctor or pharmacist now what to do in a power outage.

## Run out, or had to leave them behind?

- A **pharmacy can give you up to 3 days' supply without a prescription**
  (an "emergency supply").
- The pharmacy may also contact your doctor for a phone or electronic
  prescription.
- You may have to pay full price for these. If you can't reach a community
  pharmacy, a Queensland Health public hospital pharmacy may be able to help.

## Damaged medicines

- **Throw out medicines that touched floodwater or unsafe water**, and any that
  are **wet** or look changed.
- **Exception:** if a medicine keeps you alive and you can't get a replacement,
  you may use it **only if it looks unchanged**, and replace it as soon as
  possible.
- Heat and smoke from a fire can make medicines weaker or useless. Replace them
  if you can.
- **Refrigerated medicines that got warm** in a power outage: check the label or
  ask a pharmacist or doctor whether they're still OK. If it keeps you alive and
  there's no replacement, keep using it until you can get more.
