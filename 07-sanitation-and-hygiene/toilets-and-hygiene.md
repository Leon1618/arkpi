# Toilets and Hygiene When the Water or Sewer Fails

Diarrhoea spreads fast when toilets and handwashing stop working. It can kill,
especially young children (see
[../02-first-aid/dehydration.md](../02-first-aid/dehydration.md)).

## Wash your hands

1. Wet your hands, ideally under running water.
2. Use enough **soap** to cover them. Ordinary soap works as well as
   antibacterial soap.
3. **Rub for at least 20 seconds**: backs of hands and between the fingers.
4. Rinse, and dry them well.

**Always wash:** after the toilet or a nappy change, before preparing or eating
food, after handling raw meat, and before and after touching a wound.

**No water?** Use **alcohol-based hand sanitiser** (about half a teaspoon, rubbed
over every surface until dry, 20–30 seconds). It **doesn't work on visibly dirty
hands**. Keep it away from children: it's poisonous if swallowed.

## Toilet still there but no water

Water that isn't safe to drink (from a pool, rain or floodwater) can still be
used to **flush the toilet**, by pouring it into the bowl. Keep clean water for
drinking and cooking.

## No working toilet: bucket toilet

1. Use a **bucket lined with a strong plastic bag**.
2. Plan for about **one bag per person per day**.
3. Keep it **covered** between uses.
4. **Tie the bags off** and store them sealed, away from water and food, until
   they can be disposed of. Ask the council about collection.
5. Put a **handwashing station** (soap and water, or sanitiser) right next to it.

## Cleaning

Clean toilet seats and taps often. Use **unscented household bleach** diluted in
water. **Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners**: it makes toxic gas.
