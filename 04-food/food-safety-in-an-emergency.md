# Food Safety in an Emergency

## Power cut

- **Keep the fridge and freezer doors closed** as much as you can.
- A **fridge** keeps food cold for about **4 hours**.
- A **freezer** usually won't defrost for at least **24 hours if half full, or
  48 hours if full**.
- **Never refreeze food that has thawed.** Eat it as soon as possible, or throw
  it out.

## The 2-hour / 4-hour rule

For food that should be kept in the fridge (meat, dairy, cooked food), count the
**total time** it has been warm (between 5 °C and 60 °C):

| Total time warm | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Under 2 hours** | Put it back in the fridge, or use it |
| **2 to 4 hours** | Use it now. Don't put it back in the fridge |
| **Over 4 hours** | **Throw it out** |

## Throw it out if

- It touched **floodwater**
- It has an **unusual smell, colour or texture**
- **Don't taste it** to check. **If in doubt, throw it out.**

## Keep a supply

Keep **at least 3 days** of food and water for everyone at home, and more if you
could be cut off for longer:

- [ ] Long-life food: canned goods, long-life milk
- [ ] A manual can opener
- [ ] Bottled or stored drinking water (see [../03-water/storing-water.md](../03-water/storing-water.md))
- [ ] Food for babies and pets
- [ ] An esky with ice bricks or gel packs
- [ ] Detergent, bleach and alcohol-based hand sanitiser
