# Cyclone and Storm Tide (Noosa)

Most cyclones here come **between November and April**.

## Cyclone categories

| Cat. | Strongest gusts | Typical effects |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | under 125 km/h | Minimal house damage; damage to some trees and caravans; boats may drag moorings |
| **2** | 125–164 km/h | Minor house damage; significant damage to trees and caravans; risk of power failure |
| **3** (severe) | 165–224 km/h | Some roof and structural damage; some caravans destroyed; power failure likely |
| **4** (severe) | 225–279 km/h | Significant roof and structural damage; dangerous flying debris; widespread power failures |
| **5** (severe) | over 280 km/h | Extremely dangerous, widespread destruction |

## Before the season (October)

- Clean up the yard; trim trees and overhanging branches; clear gutters.
- Check the roof, windows and loose fittings. Fit shutters if you can.
- Know how to turn off **water, power and gas** at the mains.
- Know whether you're in a **storm tide evacuation zone** (see
  [local-hazards.md](local-hazards.md)).
- Pack your emergency kit and an evacuation kit (see
  [../11-preparedness/emergency-kit.md](../11-preparedness/emergency-kit.md)).
- Boat owners: follow Maritime Safety Queensland's severe weather plan for the
  region, double up mooring lines, and know where the boat must go.

## When a cyclone warning is issued

A warning means winds are expected **within 24 hours**. **Start your household
emergency plan now.** Depending on official advice:

- Collect children from school or childcare when told to, and go home.
- Park vehicles under solid shelter (handbrake on, in gear).
- Put outdoor furniture in the pool or inside, with other loose items.
- Close shutters, or board or tape windows (tape doesn't strengthen glass,
  but limits shattering). Draw curtains and lock doors.
- Pack your evacuation kit. **Leave if emergency services tell you to.**

## During the cyclone

- Listen to the radio: **ABC Sunshine Coast 90.3 / 95.3 FM**.
- **Stay inside, with your pets.** Unplug electrical appliances. Keep the
  fridge and freezer closed.
- Shelter in the **strongest part of the house, away from windows**: an
  internal hallway or bathroom.
- If the building starts to break up, protect yourself with mattresses, rugs
  or blankets under a strong table or bench, or hold onto something solid.
- **Beware the calm "eye".** If the wind stops, it isn't over. Violent winds
  will return from the other direction. **Wait for the official all-clear.**
- If you're driving: stop (handbrake on, in gear) **away from the sea**, trees
  and power lines, and stay in the car.

## Storm tide

A storm tide happens when a storm pushes sea water onshore on top of the normal
tide, flooding low coastal land.

- Stay inside until you're officially told it's safe.
- Don't use wet electrical appliances. Watch for fallen power lines, damaged
  bridges and trees.
- **"If it's flooded, forget it."** Don't enter floodwater.

## After

- **Don't go outside until officially advised it's safe.** If you evacuated,
  don't return until told to, and use the recommended route.
- **Check for gas leaks** (see [../01-emergency/gas-leak.md](../01-emergency/gas-leak.md))
  and fallen power lines.
- Wear rubber boots and gloves for cleanup. Treat everything that touched
  floodwater as contaminated, and wash your hands after handling it.
- Watch for damaged floors, walls and ceilings, and for **snakes**.
- Throw out food that touched floodwater, and follow any instructions about
  treating water (see [../03-water/making-water-safe.md](../03-water/making-water-safe.md)).
- Don't go sightseeing. Check on neighbours, friends and family. Avoid
  unnecessary phone calls so lines stay free.
