# Understanding Warnings (Queensland)

## Australian Warning System

Used for bushfire, flood, storm, cyclone, heat and other hazards.

| Level | Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Advice** | 🟨 Yellow | An incident has started. **No immediate danger.** Stay up to date in case it changes. |
| **Watch and Act** | 🟧 Orange | **Heightened threat.** Conditions are changing; **act now** to protect yourself and your family. |
| **Emergency Warning** | 🟥 Red | **Highest level.** You may be in danger and must **act immediately**. Any delay puts your life at risk. |

Each warning comes with an action, for example:

- *Advice:* Prepare now · Stay informed · Monitor conditions · Avoid the area · Return with caution
- *Watch and Act:* Prepare to leave · Leave now (if you are not prepared) · Move to higher ground · Do not enter flood water · Be aware of ember attack · Prepare for isolation
- *Emergency Warning:* **Leave immediately** · Shelter indoors now · **Too late / dangerous to leave**

## Fire Danger Ratings

How dangerous a fire **would be** if one started today (not a warning that
there is a fire).

| Rating | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Moderate** | Plan and prepare |
| **High** | Prepare to act |
| **Extreme** | Take action now to protect your life and property |
| **Catastrophic** | **For your survival, leave bush fire risk areas** |

Noosa is in the **Southeast Coast** fire weather district. The fire season in
South-East Queensland usually starts in **July/August** and can run into
summer.

## Other alerts

- **Standard Emergency Warning Signal (SEWS):** a **wailing siren** played at
  the start of serious warnings on radio and TV. If you hear it, listen.
- **Emergency Alert:** voice messages to landlines and text messages to mobiles
  in the affected area. Works on all carriers, with no sign-up needed.
- **Door-knocks:** emergency services may come to your door with warnings.
- **Noosa ALERT:** the council's opt-in SMS and email alerts. Sign up at
  https://disaster.noosa.qld.gov.au

When you get a warning: **act immediately**, make sure everyone in the house
knows, check on neighbours who may need help, and start your household
emergency plan.
