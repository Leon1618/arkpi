# Emergency Communication

## Calling for help

- **000** for police, fire and ambulance. **112** also works from a mobile.
- If you can't speak English, call 000 and say "police", "fire" or
  "ambulance". They will arrange a free interpreter.
- The **Emergency+** app shows your exact location (latitude and longitude) to
  read out to the 000 operator. Install it now.
- **SES (storm and flood help): 132 500.**
- Full local list: [../12-noosa-tewantin/emergency-contacts.md](../12-noosa-tewantin/emergency-contacts.md)

## When phones and internet are down

- **NBN phones and internet stop working in a power cut**, even with a backup at
  home. Keep a mobile and a power bank charged.
- **Avoid unnecessary calls** during a disaster, to keep the lines free. Send a
  text instead.
- **Battery or wind-up radio:** **ABC Sunshine Coast 90.3 FM or 95.3 FM** is the
  emergency broadcaster. Listen for the **emergency warning siren** played
  before serious warnings.

## UHF CB radio (no licence needed)

| Channel | Use |
|---|---|
| **UHF 5** (476.525 MHz) | **Emergencies only** |
| **UHF 35** (477.275 MHz) | Emergencies only |
| HF 9 (27.065 MHz) | Emergencies only |

- These channels may be used **during an emergency or natural disaster**.
- In an emergency you can call on **any** CB channel to get attention.
- **Emergency services don't listen to these channels.** CB doesn't replace
  calling 000; use it to reach people nearby.

## Signalling for help

- **Trapped (e.g. in rubble):** **bang on a pipe or wall**, send a text, and use
  a **whistle** instead of shouting. Cover your mouth with your shirt.
- **Trapped in a building (e.g. fire):** signal at a window with a
  **light-coloured cloth or a torch**.
- **On a roof in a flood:** stay visible and signal to rescuers.
- **Outdoors:** a fire can be seen from the air. Rotten or damp wood makes lots
  of smoke, which is useful for signalling (see
  [../06-fire-and-light/lighting-a-fire.md](../06-fire-and-light/lighting-a-fire.md)).
